why a good curve isn't an edge

Three ways your backtest lies — quietly.

None of them show up in the equity curve. That's the whole problem: the curve looks the same whether the edge is real or an artifact. Each of these killed strategies of mine that looked, right up until the test, like winners.

01

Fills you will never get

Your simulator fills at the signal price. The real market gaps straight through your stop and hands you the next print. My strongest strategy measured a 2.23 profit factor at zero cost — and 1.06 once each hedged round trip paid the spread four times. Friction, not the idea, was the murderer.

02

A universe that already survived

Backtesting on the tickers that exist today quietly deletes every company that went to zero. The dead names were the whole risk. The same code scored 1.47 on a curated universe and 1.05 on a de-survivorship one — the difference was pure selection bias.

03

Goalposts that moved

The parameters got chosen after you saw the result. Nothing in the curve will ever tell you that happened — which is exactly why every gate here was written down before the test ran, and left untouched after. Pre-register, or you're writing fiction.
